Key expansion

Why AES expands the key

AES expands one 128-bit key into 44 words: four words for each round key. Every fourth word uses RotWord, SubWord, and Rcon.

128-bit key → 44 words in 11 groups of 4 (one round key per group).

Word

4 bytes, shown as 8 hex characters.

Round key

Four words form one 128-bit round key.

Schedule

Later keys are generated from earlier words.

Standard AES-128 key schedule formulas
Initial words
w[0], w[1], w[2], w[3]

The original 128-bit key is split into four 32-bit words.

Normal word
w[i] = w[i-4] XOR w[i-1]

This is used when i is not a multiple of 4.

Every fourth word
w[i] = w[i-4] XOR SubWord(RotWord(w[i-1])) XOR Rcon[i/4]

This adds byte rotation, S-box substitution, and the round constant.

RotWordmoves the first byte to the end, for example aabbccdd becomes bbccddaa.
SubWordreplaces each byte using the S-box table.
Rconis XORed into the first byte of every fourth generated word.
